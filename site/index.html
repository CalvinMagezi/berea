A study Bible that shows its work.

Berea is a free Bible and study assistant that runs entirely on your own phone or computer. Every answer is built from the Bible text, the Hebrew and Greek behind it and the passages it points to, then checked against that text before you see it.

No account, no tracking, and nothing you write leaves your device. This page explains how it works, so you can examine it the way the Bereans examined Paul.

What Berea promises

  • Nothing about you is collected.

    No account, no analytics, no crash reports, no sync. Your notes, studies and highlights live in one file on your device.

  • It works with no connection.

    The Bible, the Hebrew and Greek, the lexicon, the cross references and the language model are all on the device. After the one-time model download, flight mode is fine.

  • Scripture comes first.

    The model never quotes from memory. It is given the verses and told to use only those, and every reference in its answer is looked up afterwards. Anything it made up is marked, not hidden.

  • Everything is open.

    The app, the data pipeline and the tests are published under the MIT licence. The Bible text and study data are public domain or openly licensed.

The promise is enforced in code, not policy. The only network calls are the ones you start: the model download, the optional lexicon download, and, on a Mac, your own Ollama server. See app_state.dart

The app

Three places: Study for questions, Bible for reading, Notes for everything you keep. A chat button is on every screen.

How an answer is made

Small language models that fit on a phone are fluent but forgetful. Asked to quote the Bible from memory they will sometimes invent a verse, merge two, or cite a book that does not exist. Berea is built so that this does not depend on the model being good. Every question goes through the same five steps. The panel replays one real question through them.

  1. References are read from your question in every common form (John 3:16, Jn 3.16-18, 1 Cor 13, II Timothy 3:16) and the verses are fetched word for word from the Bible on your device. ref_parser.dart

  2. The Hebrew or Greek words with their dictionary entries, the passages other readers most often link to it, and a search of the whole Bible for your question's words. A question with no reference follows those links to the passages Scripture keeps returning to. retriever.dart

  3. The model receives a fixed set of study rules and the gathered passages, trimmed to what it can hold. It is told to quote only from them and to say plainly when something is not in the Bible. system.md

  4. On a phone the answer is written by Gemma, Google's open model, through the same engine as Google AI Edge Gallery. On a Mac it can use Ollama instead. edge_engine.dart

  5. A chapter, verse or book that does not exist is marked unverified. A quotation that is not word for word is replaced with the exact words when one verse is clearly its source, and marked otherwise. Verified references become links that open the passage. citation_guard.dart

What the check catches

Here is the last step at work on a deliberately bad answer: a misquoted verse and a book that does not exist. What Berea shows is the check's real output for it.

The rules also ask the model to say plainly when a saying is not in the Bible. A test set of 40 questions runs before every release, eight of them deliberately misleading: a chapter that does not exist, Psalm 151, “the verse that says God helps those who help themselves”. The questions and the scoring are public in golden.json and eval.dart.

No check is perfect. A model can still misread a passage it was given. The aim is that it cannot quietly put words in Scripture's mouth.

Your data, exactly

Here is everything Berea stores and everything it sends.

Stored on your device

  • The Bible library described above.
  • One file for what you write: notes, highlights, saved studies, study pages and reading plan progress.
  • Translations you import, and your settings.

Sent over the internet

  • A request to Hugging Face for the language model, once, when you tap Download.
  • A request to GitHub for the optional Greek lexicon, only if you tap Download. Its fingerprint is checked before it is used.
  • On a Mac using Ollama, your questions go to the Ollama address you set, which is your own computer unless you change it.

Nothing else. No analytics, no crash reports, no account, no sync.

Backups

Berea opts out of Android and iCloud backups on purpose, so your notes never sit on someone else's server. The cost is that a lost or reset phone loses them unless you keep a Berea backup: one file, encrypted with a passphrase only you know, saved wherever you choose. Without the passphrase nobody can open it, including you.

AES-256-GCM with a key derived from your passphrase (PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds): backup.dart. Android backup opt-out: data_extraction_rules.xml.

Which model runs

Berea reads how much memory your device has and recommends a model. A larger model writes better answers; the checks are the same for all of them.

  • Gemma 4 E2B

    2.6 GB

    Most phones, 5 to 10 GB of memory.

  • Gemma 4 E4B

    4.3 GB

    Phones and computers with 10 GB or more.

  • Gemma 3 1B

    0.5 GB

    Phones with less memory. Needs a free Hugging Face token.

On a phone's processor alone, a first answer takes about a minute on a mid-range device; a phone's graphics chip is much faster. These numbers come from an emulator and will be updated from real devices.

Try it

  1. 1

    On a 64-bit Android phone running Android 11 or later, tap Download for Android.

  2. 2

    Open the downloaded file. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser; allow it for this install.

  3. 3

    Open Berea, tap the settings icon, and tap Download for the model (2.6 GB, best on Wi-Fi). From then on it works offline.

Download for Android Release notes

Berea is not on Google Play yet. The download is signed by the project, and later versions install over it. iPhone, Mac, Windows and Linux build from the source today with the build guide. Found a wrong answer? Open an issue; questions that trip the model become new test cases.
