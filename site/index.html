A study Bible that shows its work.

Free, offline and private. Every answer is checked against the Bible text before you see it.

Ask a small language model to quote the Bible from memory.

Sometimes it invents a verse.

Sometimes it merges two into one.

Sometimes it cites 2 Hezekiah 4:1, a book that is not in the Bible.

Berea is built so that none of this depends on the model getting it right.

Everything it reads is on your phone.

Three translations, 31,102 verses each. Every Hebrew and Greek word behind them, 447,319 in all, with 21,232 dictionary entries and 344,799 cross references ranked by how often readers follow them.

The Bible opens first, with nothing to download; the first run needs about 85 MB of free space. The language model for Study is optional and comes down once, on Wi-Fi unless you allow mobile data, and carries on where it stopped if the connection drops: Gemma 4 E2B (2.6 GB) on most phones, E4B (3.7 GB) on devices with 10 GB of memory or more, and the 0.6 GB Gemma 3 1B on phones under 5 GB. After that, flight mode is fine.

How an answer is made

One real question, as Berea handled it.

  1. 1Find the passages you named.
  2. 2Gather what a careful reader would open.
  3. 3Hand the model only that material.
  4. 4Let the model write.
  5. 5Check every reference and quotation.

Press a word. See the word under it.

Long-press perfect in Matthew 5:48 and Berea opens τέλειος, teleios: what it means, all 19 of its uses across 9 books, how each is translated, and every verse.

One English word can stand for several. The King James Version’s perfect in Job 1:1 is תָּם, tam, which the Berean Standard Bible translates blameless. In the Berean Standard Bible each word is matched exactly, from its translators’ own tables; in other translations Berea matches by meaning and says so. Ask Berea about the word and the answer is built from its other uses, then checked like any other.

    Your data, exactly.

    Everything Berea stores, and everything it sends.

    Stored on your phone

    The Bible library above. One file for what you write: notes and the notebooks they sit in, the readings you add to a notebook, highlights, saved studies, reading plans and prayers. Translations you import, and your settings.

    Sent over the internet

    The model, once, from Hugging Face (or from GitHub for the smallest model), when you tap Download. The optional Greek lexicon from GitHub, checked against its fingerprint. On a Mac with Ollama, your questions go to the Ollama address you set. A prayer or a note leaves only if you share it, through your phone's own share sheet. Nothing else: no analytics, no crash reports, no account, no sync.

    Backups

    Berea opts out of Android and iCloud backup on purpose. Keep your own backup instead: one file with your notes, notebooks, readings and prayers, encrypted with a passphrase only you know (AES-256-GCM, PBKDF2 at 600,000 rounds).
